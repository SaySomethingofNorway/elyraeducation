Key requirement for every area

Plug and play.

Every solution we choose must be easy to set up with low effort, so we can open classrooms in many villages at the same time. Prefer complete kits over custom builds.

  • Set up in hours by one trained local person, without specialists on site.
  • Same standard kit in every village: screen, power, internet and platform.
  • Pre-configured before shipping and managed remotely after installation.
  • New classrooms can be added in parallel without extra central work.

Elyra Earth Education

A world-class classroom in every village.

Elyra Earth brings top educators into village schools through digital lessons, supported by local teachers on site. The goal is human capital: the knowledge and skills a country needs while it builds prosperity.

See how it works Partner with us
Why we build it this way

Talent is everywhere. Good teaching is not.

Education quality in the regions where Elyra works is mixed, and often poor. Classrooms lack trained teachers, materials and a curriculum connected to the work a growing economy needs.

We do not try to fix this one teacher at a time. Every child in an Elyra village with a school or education programme gets the same top-quality lessons, delivered digitally by leading educators and supported in the room by local teachers.

The education is secular, built on science and facts, and practical. It is designed around one question: what do the people of this country need to know to build it, run it and lead it?

The investment case

Education is one of the smartest investments the world can make.

$65per $1

Benefit for every dollar spent on improving how children learn in low- and lower-middle-income countries. Copenhagen Consensus Center, Best Things First (Bjørn Lomborg, 2023).

The Copenhagen Consensus Center asked leading economists to rank the world's most effective development investments. Better education came out near the top, with an estimated $65 in social and economic benefit for every dollar spent.

The return comes from what children actually learn, not only how many years they sit in school. Children who learn more become more productive adults, earn more and contribute more to their communities and their country.

It is also an investment in the environment and climate. Educated, more prosperous societies farm more sustainably, adopt cleaner technology and are better able to protect their land and adapt to a changing climate. That is why Elyra sees education as the foundation for a sustainable future.

AssumptionThe income figures are Elyra planning assumptions for a top performer, not measured outcomes. They should be checked against Kenyan earnings data (KNBS) before use with investors.

How it is calculated. Extra income = professional income minus income without the education, both growing at the same real rate, over the working years after education ends. The return counts only the person's own extra income. It leaves out taxes paid, jobs created for others and wider effects on the community, so the full return to the country is likely higher.

Why two numbers. The first divides total extra income by total cost. The discounted figure values money received decades from now lower than money spent today, which is how economists, including the Copenhagen Consensus, compare investments.

Five layers make one classroom work.

Read from the bottom up. Each layer depends on the one below it.

Digital education from top educators

The best teachers reach every Elyra classroom at once. A child in a rural village gets the same lesson as a child in a capital city, at the same quality.

Local teachers as assistants and helpers

Local teachers know the children, the language and the community. They lead the room, support the lesson and make sure no one is left behind. The role also builds teaching skills locally.

Starlink, power and equipment

High-speed internet through Starlink, stable power and a large interactive screen are standard in every classroom. Without them, digital education does not happen.

Safety comes first

Safe buildings, protected equipment and Elyra's safeguarding rules apply in every classroom, including guardian consent and strict media rules for any content showing children.

Two tracks, equal standing

The workshop is as important as the library.

A country building prosperity needs scientists and economists. It also needs mechanics, electricians, welders, food producers, cooks and designers. Every child should find a path that fits them.
